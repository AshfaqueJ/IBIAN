The Visionaries

The extraordinary individuals who dared to imagine — and built the cornerstone of IBIAN Club’s enduring legacy.

Architects of Excellence

In the autumn of 1978, three remarkable trailblazers united with a singular vision: to create a sanctuary where brilliance, culture, and camaraderie would flourish. Their diverse genius forged the soul of IBIAN Club, and their spirit resonates in every hall to this day.

✦ CO-FOUNDER & PATRON OF THE ARTS ✦

Eleanor Blake

“Where culture meets intention.”

A philanthropist and former gallery owner with an unerring eye for beauty, Eleanor Blake brought artistic soul to the IBIAN foundation. Her belief that a private club must nourish the creative spirit led to the inauguration of the annual IBIAN Art Salon and the acquisition of the club's legendary collection of postwar masterpieces. Eleanor personally curated the inaugural exhibition in 1979, featuring emerging talents who would become icons. Her legendary soirees in the East Wing drew luminaries from Hollywood to the Louvre, forging IBIAN's reputation as a nexus for creative minds. Eleanor's guiding principle — "Excellence is an art, not an accident" — remains etched above the club's main hearth.

“I envisioned a place where a painter and a financier could share a whiskey and discover common genius. That spark — that unexpected collision of worlds — is the heartbeat of IBIAN.”
✦ CO-FOUNDER & STRATEGIC ARCHITECT ✦

Benjamin Montclair

“Legacy is built on quiet resolve.”

A former diplomat and international financier, Benjamin Montclair was the strategic mastermind behind IBIAN's global expansion and membership ethos. He drafted the club's original constitution, emphasizing discretion, mutual respect, and global citizenship. Benjamin's network brought the first wave of international founding members — ambassadors, Nobel laureates, and industrialists — transforming a local idea into a worldwide institution. His stewardship of the club's investment portfolio ensured financial independence for decades. Montclair's weekly "Global Affairs Breakfasts" became a signature tradition, where world events were debated with civility and foresight. His quiet diplomacy earned IBIAN the nickname "The Chamber of Goodwill."

“A club is not built on marble, but on the quality of its conversations. We sought to create a living room for the world’s finest minds — a place where trust is the currency and curiosity the compass.”
✦ CO-FOUNDER & CUSTODIAN OF TRADITION ✦

Sir Marcus Reeves

“Elegance endures.”

A revered historian and former royal curator, Sir Marcus Reeves infused IBIAN with timeless rituals and architectural splendor. He oversaw the restoration of the original neo-Georgian mansion, personally selecting every cornice, chandelier, and leather binding. Marcus authored the club's ceremonial traditions — from the Founder's Toast to the Winter Gala's candlelight procession — ensuring that each event carried weight and meaning. His library, containing over 5,000 rare volumes, remains a sanctuary for members seeking quiet reflection. Sir Marcus believed that a club must honor the past while embracing the future, and his influence can be seen in every hand-carved detail of the Great Hall.

“We are but temporary guardians of a grand tradition. The walls remember every toast, every debate, every silence shared among friends. My duty was to ensure those walls spoke of grace and permanence.”

Beyond the three founders, eighty-four distinguished patrons answered the inaugural call — pioneers in medicine, literature, enterprise, and the arts. Together, they formed a brotherhood and sisterhood of excellence that set the standard for generations. Their names are inscribed on the bronze Honor Roll in the Founder’s Corridor, a lasting tribute to the courageous few who believed in a radical vision of hospitality and human connection.

What Eleanor, Benjamin, and Marcus built was never just a club — it was a living philosophy. They dreamed of a place where ambition found grace, where conversations changed lives, and where every member felt they belonged to something eternal. Their legacy is our daily compass.
— From the IBIAN Charter, 1978
