25 Years of Bengali Elegance

The Dhaka Royale Gala

Where the Soul of Bengal Met the Pinnacle of Luxury

On the 14th of December, 2024, IBIAN Dhaka turned twenty-five — and the city of mosques and rivers paused to celebrate with us. This is the story of the most magnificent night in Bangladeshi social history.

✦ ✦ ✦

“Not a party — a celebration of Bengal.”

Three hundred and fifty distinguished guests in exquisite Jamdani and silk, eighty-seven bottles of the finest Dom Pérignon flown in from Épernay, three living legends of Bangladeshi music, and a grand ballroom transformed into a dreamscape of Shapla (water lilies) and golden Kashar (brass). The IBIAN Club’s 25th Anniversary Gala wasn’t merely an event — it was a declaration that Dhaka now stands shoulder-to-shoulder with the great capitals of the world.

350 Distinguished Guests
15 Course Bengali Feast
3 Living Legends (Music)
$1.8M Raised for Artisan Welfare
The Arrival

A Red Carpet Woven with Jamdani

At precisely 7:00 PM, the Grand Porte-Cochère of the IBIAN Dhaka House transformed into a crimson and gold corridor reminiscent of the Mughal era. Members arrived in chauffeur-driven Mercedes and BMWs — but the real spectacle was the attire. Jamdani sarees with gold threadwork, Panjabis crafted from Dhaka muslin, and Nakshi Kantha stoles that told stories of rural Bengal. The paparazzi from Daily Star Life, Ice Today, and Vogue India captured every glittering moment.

The dress code was “Royal Bengal Noir”: a fusion of traditional Bangladeshi craftsmanship and contemporary high fashion. One member arrived in a vintage Rolls-Royce that had once carried a Nawab of Dhaka — because at IBIAN, we don't just attend events; we arrive with history.

“I have walked red carpets in Paris and New York, but nothing — absolutely nothing — compares to the sheer majesty of that IBIAN Dhaka entrance. It felt like stepping into a Tagore poem set to a modern symphony.” — Dr. Shahiduzzaman Khan, Patron Member since 2009
The Cathedral Ballroom

Where Light Became Art — and Bengal

The Cathedral Ballroom of IBIAN Dhaka — already legendary for its 40-foot ceilings and colonial-era archways — was utterly reimagined for the anniversary. A cascading chandelier of 10,000 crystals descended from the dome, but it was the floating Shapla (water lilies) in brass urns and the massive Nakshi Kantha tapestry draped behind the stage that stole the show. 120 round tables draped in ivory and green silk, each centered with a cascading arrangement of marigolds, roses, and orchids flown in from the Sundarbans.

The stage was a masterpiece: a golden proscenium arch inspired by the Lalbagh Fort, flanked by 15-foot LED screens that displayed live footage of the evening’s magic. The orchestra — a 28-piece ensemble blending classical Western instruments with the esraj, harmonium, and tabla — struck the first chord at 8:15 PM, and the room rose as one.

“I have been a member since the Dhaka chapter opened. I thought I had seen it all. Then I walked into that ballroom and I wept. It was not just beautiful — it was Bengali. It was us.” — Ms. Farhana Ahsan, Founding Member, Dhaka
The Gastronomic Odyssey

Fifteen Courses of Bengali Brilliance

The menu was the magnum opus of Chef Tahmina Akhter and her brigade of twenty-two. Each course was a love letter to Bengal's culinary heritage, paired with rare vintages from the club's 2,500-bottle cellar, curated by our Master Sommelier, Jean-Pierre Dubois. From the opening amuse-bouche — a delicate Fuchka (golgappa) with spiced tamarind water and a hint of gold leaf — to the final dessert — a Gurer Payesh (date jaggery rice pudding) with pistachio foam — every bite was a revelation.

The pièce de résistance was the main course: a Mutton Rezala from the royal kitchens of Old Dhaka, paired with a 1998 Château Margaux that brought the room to a reverent hush. One member later called it “the best meal of my life — and I have dined at every three-star in Europe.”

The Stage

Three Legends, One Unforgettable Night

The entertainment was nothing short of historic. At 10:00 PM, the legendary Runa Laila took the stage for a spellbinding 45-minute set — her voice weaving through classics like “Aaj Shonar Bangladesh” and “Chand Ki Tarah” as the chandelier dimmed to a warm amber glow. Members swayed, some with tears, as she dedicated a song to the spirit of Dhaka.

At midnight, the room erupted as Shafin Ahmed (of Miles) launched into a high-energy medley of rock, folk, and soul that had even the most reserved members on their feet. The grand finale came at 1:30 AM: a surprise appearance by Rezwana Choudhury Bannya, who performed a soul-stirring rendition of “O Amar Desher Mati” from the mezzanine balcony as the chandelier rained golden confetti over the crowd. Three hundred and fifty people wept — unashamedly, joyfully, together — in a moment of pure Bengali pride.

“I have performed for heads of state, for the Queen, for audiences around the globe. But that night at IBIAN Dhaka — the energy, the love, the sheer Bangaliana in that room — it was unlike anything I have ever experienced. I will carry it in my heart forever.” — Runa Laila, in a letter to the Club
“The Dhaka Royale Gala wasn’t just a party — it was a reclamation. A reclamation of Bengali elegance on the world stage. I left feeling like I had been part of something that will be spoken of for generations.”
— Mahfuz Anam Member since 2015
“The food was divine. The music was transcendent. But what I will remember most is the feeling of belonging. At IBIAN Dhaka, you are not a guest. You are family — a family that spans generations.”
— Mr. Latifur Rahman Patron Member, 15 years
“I have attended galas in London, Dubai, and Singapore. The IBIAN 25th Anniversary Gala in Dhaka was the single most magnificent event I have ever witnessed. The fusion of global luxury with Bengali soul was perfection.”
— Shajeeb Wazed Honorary Member, London
